## Summary & Key Takeaways

- Google introduced Gemini 4 Neon, adding to the existing Hydrogen, Argon, and Carbon lineup.
- The pricing structure for Gemini 4 models is criticized for being increasingly confusing, especially with introductory discounts.
- Neon is currently the most expensive of the three new models, but pricing will shift on December 1st.
- Simon Willison conducted practical tests of Neon's reasoning levels using "pelicans" (a common benchmark).
- Neon's performance appears to be better than Hydrogen but not as good as Carbon.
- An example prompt for a Three.js animation was tested against Gemini 4 Neon.

## Our Commentary

Google's LLM naming and pricing strategy is becoming a genuine mess. Simon Willison's breakdown here is essential because it cuts through the marketing fluff and shows the real-world implications. I genuinely don't know how they expect developers to navigate this labyrinth of tiers and temporary discounts. It feels like they're making it harder, not easier, to adopt their models.
