## Summary & Key Takeaways

- Coding agents have significantly reduced the effort required for reverse-engineering.
- The traditional high ROI barrier for automating undocumented APIs has been lowered.
- Developers are now more willing to attempt and discard automation projects due to reduced initial cost.
- This shift makes previously impractical home device automation projects viable.
- The article highlights how AI changes the psychological baggage associated with code maintenance and initial development.

## Our Commentary

Simon Willison hits it out of the park again. "Reverse-engineering is cheap now" is such a potent statement. I've definitely felt that friction when considering automating some obscure device. The idea that the _cost of trying and failing_ has dropped is huge. It's not just about writing code faster; it's about changing the entire risk-reward calculation for personal projects. This is a genuine paradigm shift for hobbyists and tinkerers.
