## Summary & Key Takeaways

• Zach Kehs states that physical buildings have limits to growth before collapse.
• He contrasts this with software, which lacks such inherent constraints.
• Software code can always be made worse, indefinitely.
• This degradation can manifest as new layers of indirection.
• It can also lead to a reduction in performance.
• The quote offers a stark perspective on technical debt.

## Our Commentary

This quote is a gut punch, isn't it? "The code can always get worse." It's a dark truth about our craft. We often talk about technical debt as something to be paid down, but this suggests it's an ever-present, potentially infinite force. It makes me feel a bit helpless, honestly. How do you fight something that has no natural limit?
