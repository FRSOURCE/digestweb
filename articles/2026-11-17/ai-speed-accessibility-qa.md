## Summary & Key Takeaways

- AI has significantly increased the speed of design and build processes.
- Quality assurance (QA) testing, particularly for accessibility, struggles to keep pace.
- This imbalance may lead to a less accessible web.
- New research identifies practices of teams successfully maintaining accessibility standards.
- The article provides guidance on integrating human judgment into AI-enabled workflows.
- The goal is to ensure accessibility without compromising delivery speed.
- It highlights the importance of balancing automation with human oversight in AI-driven development.

## Our Commentary

This article hits on a really important, and frankly, unsettling point. We're all chasing speed with AI, but if we're sacrificing accessibility in the process, what's the point? I've seen this tension firsthand. The idea of embedding human judgment into AI workflows is crucial; it's not about replacing humans, but augmenting them responsibly. This is a conversation we absolutely need to keep having as AI becomes more ingrained in our development cycles.
