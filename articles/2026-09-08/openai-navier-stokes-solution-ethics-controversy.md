## Summary & Key Takeaways

- OpenAI claims to have resolved the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem, a Millennium Prize Problem.
- This achievement is clouded by accusations from Tristan Buckmaster, who was working on the same problem with Levent Alpöge (Anthropic).
- Buckmaster alleges OpenAI prompted their model after hearing rumors of his team's breakthrough.
- OpenAI reportedly refused to include Alpöge as a co-author due to competitive reasons with Anthropic.
- The incident raises serious questions about AI research ethics, intellectual property, and competitive practices.

## Our Commentary

This is wild. A Millennium Prize Problem, potentially solved by AI, but the whole thing is steeped in accusations of corporate espionage and competitive backstabbing. I mean, seriously? The idea that AI models are now racing to "solve" problems based on rumors of human progress is deeply unsettling. And the refusal to co-author because of company rivalry? It feels like the worst aspects of human competition are being amplified by AI's capabilities. This is a mess, and I'm not sure how we untangle it.
