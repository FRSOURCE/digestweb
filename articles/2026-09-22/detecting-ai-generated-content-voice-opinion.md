## Summary & Key Takeaways

- The article quotes @therealcornpop on the detectability of AI-generated content.
- AI-written scripts often exhibit predictable patterns, or 'AI-isms'.
- These patterns include generic phrasing, the 'rule of three,' and staccato writing.
- A key indicator is the absence of a definitive personal voice or genuine opinions.
- The commentary suggests AI content often lacks depth and authenticity.

## Our Commentary

This hits home. I've definitely noticed the 'AI-isms' in content, and @therealcornpop nails it: it's the _lack_ of a definitive voice. It's not just about grammar or structure; it's the absence of genuine human messiness and conviction. It makes me wonder how long until models can truly fake that, or if it's an inherent limitation. For now, it's a useful heuristic for spotting the bots.
